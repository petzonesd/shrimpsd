Quarantining new shrimp

A small, separate tank gives you time to watch new shrimp before they join your colony. It is cheap insurance for a tank you have worked hard on.

The short answer

In one line

Keep new shrimp in a separate tank with their own equipment for a couple of weeks, watch how they behave, and only then move them to your main tank.

Shrimp are shipped, handled and moved between tanks, which is stressful, and stress can bring out problems that were hidden. Quarantine is not a guarantee, and many keepers skip it without trouble. But if you already have a thriving colony, a few extra weeks of watching is a small price compared with the risk of losing it.

A simple quarantine setup

The setup does not need to be fancy. A small tank or a clean plastic tub, a gentle sponge filter, a heater if your room needs one, and a few strands of moss or a plant for the shrimp to hold onto will do. Use dechlorinated water that suits the type you are keeping: Neocaridina handle treated tap water, while Caridina need soft, acidic water, see Neocaridina vs Caridina and San Diego tap water. Match the temperature and water to what the shrimp are used to, and acclimate them slowly when you first add them.

A sponge filter seeded from your main tank, or a tank that has already finished cycling, helps keep the water steady. Keep a separate net and tools for this tank, or rinse them well between uses, and wash your hands between tanks.

What to watch for

Over the first days and weeks, look for shrimp that are active, graze on surfaces, eat when you feed lightly and molt without trouble (see molting). Warning signs include shrimp that sit motionless, lose colour suddenly, stop eating or die without an obvious cause. A death or two right after arrival can happen from transit stress, so judge the trend over time rather than a single loss. If several die, review the checklist in why shrimp die and test the water.

Medicating and moving on

We would not treat shrimp preventively. Many common fish remedies are harmful to shrimp, and copper in particular can be deadly, see medications and copper. If you are worried about something, ask us before adding anything.

Once the new shrimp have behaved normally for a couple of weeks, acclimate them again to the main tank and move them across. Do not pour quarantine water into the main tank, and add only the shrimp. Keep an eye on the whole colony for the next few days.

Questions we get asked

Do I need to quarantine new shrimp?

It is a good habit, especially if you already have a healthy colony you care about. A separate tank lets you watch new arrivals for problems before they can share anything with your established shrimp. Shrimp from a trusted source that look active and healthy are lower risk, but the risk is never zero.

How long should I quarantine shrimp?

There is no single proven number. Many keepers watch new shrimp for a couple of weeks, and some go longer. The goal is to see them eat, graze, molt and behave normally, with no unexplained deaths, before moving them.

What equipment does a quarantine tank need?

Something small and simple: a tank or tub, a gentle sponge filter, a heater if your room needs one, a few plants or moss to hold onto, and water matched to the shrimp. Keep nets and tools separate, or rinse them well, so nothing travels between tanks.

Should I medicate shrimp in quarantine?

We would not medicate by default. Many fish medications, especially anything containing copper, can kill shrimp. If you think something is wrong, read the label carefully and ask us before adding anything to the tank.

Can I quarantine shrimp in the same tank as my other shrimp?

Not in a meaningful way. Quarantine only works when the new shrimp are in separate water with separate equipment. Adding a divider inside the same tank still shares the water.
