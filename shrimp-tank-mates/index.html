Shrimp tank mates — what is safe, and what eats shrimp

The honest rule first: almost any fish will eat baby shrimp. The real question is whether you want a growing colony or just some shrimp in a community tank. Answer that, and the list below sorts itself out.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

The short answer

In one line

If you want the colony to multiply, keep shrimp species-only with snails. If you just want shrimp in a community tank, choose fish small enough that an adult shrimp will not fit in their mouth, and give the babies dense moss to hide in.

Safe with adult shrimp

SnailsNerite, mystery, ramshorn and bladder snails are the best tank mates there are — they share the work and leave shrimp alone. The one exception is the assassin snail, which hunts shrimp.
OtocinclusPeaceful algae grazers that ignore shrimp. Buy them for an established tank with plenty of biofilm.
Small corydorasPygmy, habrosus and hastatus corydoras are small and gentle. They may pick at a shrimplet they stumble on, but they do not hunt.
Tiny rasborasChili and other Boraras rasboras, and similar micro fish, are about as shrimp-safe as schooling fish get. Some shrimplets will still be eaten.
Amano shrimpLive alongside Neocaridina and Caridina without crossbreeding. They are bigger and bolder at feeding time, so spread the food. See Amano shrimp.

Risky — depends on the individual

BettasSome ignore shrimp, some hunt every one. You will not know until you try, so only try with cheap shrimp and lots of cover.
Small tetrasEmber and neon tetras usually leave adults alone but eat shrimplets readily.
Guppies, endlers and other livebearersGenerally fine with adults, very good at finding babies.
Kuhli loachesPeaceful, but they forage in the same places shrimplets hide.

Do not mix with shrimp

Anything with a mouth big enough to fit an adult shrimp will eventually eat one. That includes cichlids of every kind (rams and apistogramma included), angelfish, gouramis, goldfish, larger barbs and tetras, most loaches, crayfish, crabs and pea puffers, which eat shrimp and snails as a staple. Assassin snails also belong on this list.

Shrimp with other shrimp

Different Neocaridina colors will crossbreed. Red cherry, blue dream, yellow and the rest are all one species. Mixed together, within a few generations the offspring revert toward brown wild-type. If you want to keep a color, keep one color per tank. More in the Neocaridina guide.

Neocaridina and Caridina should not share a tank, but not because of breeding — they need opposite water, so one group is always stressed. See Neocaridina vs Caridina.

Amano shrimp mix with either, since they cannot breed in freshwater at all. Filter feeders such as bamboo and vampire shrimp are peaceful too, but need a larger tank with steady flow and are not a fit for most small shrimp setups.

How to make a community tank work

  • Cover, in quantity. Java or christmas moss and dense plants give shrimplets places fish cannot reach.
  • Add shrimp first. A colony that is established before fish arrive survives far better than shrimp added to a tank fish already own.
  • Start with enough shrimp. Ten to fifteen gives the colony room to absorb some losses.
  • Feed the fish well. Hungry fish hunt; fed fish mostly do not.

Questions we get asked

What fish can live with cherry shrimp?

Otocinclus, small corydoras such as pygmy and habrosus, and micro fish such as chili rasboras are the safest choices. Snails other than assassin snails are ideal. Most fish will still eat some baby shrimp, so add moss for cover.

Can shrimp live with a betta?

Sometimes. Some bettas ignore shrimp and others hunt them, and there is no way to tell in advance. If you try it, use inexpensive shrimp, plenty of moss and plants, and accept that you may lose them.

Can different colors of cherry shrimp live together?

They can live together safely, but they will crossbreed because they are all the same species. Within a few generations the babies revert toward brown wild-type. Keep one color per tank if you want to keep the color.

Can Amano shrimp live with cherry shrimp?

Yes. Amano shrimp cannot breed in freshwater, so there is no crossbreeding, and they are peaceful. They are larger and pushier at feeding time, so spread food around the tank.

Do snails eat shrimp?

Common aquarium snails such as nerite, mystery, ramshorn and bladder snails do not. Assassin snails do hunt shrimp and should not be kept with them.
