Care guide

Why aren’t my shrimp breeding?

Most stalled colonies come down to age, group makeup, steady water or stress. Here is what to check, in order.

The short answer

In one line

Shrimp breed when they are mature, healthy and settled in steady water with food and cover. If a tank is not producing berried females, the cause is usually age, a recent move, unstable water or stress, and patience plus a steadier tank fixes most of it.

Neocaridina breed readily in the right conditions. Caridina are slower and fussier. Either way, a colony that is calm and well fed will usually breed without any special trick, so the job is to remove what is getting in the way.

What to check, in order

1. Age. Young shrimp are not ready to breed and take a few months to mature. If your group is small and juvenile, the answer may simply be time. See how long shrimp live.

2. Who is in the tank. You need adult females and adult males. A mixed group of adults will pair up on its own, so there is no ratio to manage. If you are unsure what you have, see male vs female shrimp.

3. Recent changes. A new tank, a move, or a bad acclimation can pause breeding for a while. Give shrimp a few weeks to settle before you judge them. See how to acclimate shrimp.

4. Water stability. Swings in temperature or water chemistry stress shrimp more than a slightly imperfect but steady tank does. Keep water changes small and regular, see water changes, and check the right range for your species in water parameters.

5. The right water for the species. Neocaridina cope with dechlorinated tap. Caridina need soft, acidic, low-mineral water, and often stall in the wrong water, see Neocaridina vs Caridina and RO water for shrimp.

6. Food. Hungry or poorly fed shrimp do not berry well. Offer small, varied meals and let biofilm build up, but do not overfeed, see feeding shrimp.

7. Stress and company. Fish that harass or eat shrimp, constant disturbance, and poor cover all hold breeding back. See tank mates and why shrimp hide.

8. Hidden problems. Copper, other contamination or a poorly cycled tank can quietly suppress a colony. See medications and copper and cycling a shrimp tank.

Is it really not breeding?

Check before you worry. Shrimplets are tiny and hide in moss and plants, so a colony can be breeding for weeks before you see a baby. Look for berried females and for very small shrimp grazing on leaves. See berried shrimp and breeding for what to expect once eggs appear.

Amano shrimp

If your shrimp are Amano, they will not breed in a freshwater tank, because the larvae need brackish water. This is normal and not a sign that anything is wrong. See Amano shrimp.

Questions we get asked

Why are my shrimp not breeding?

The usual causes are shrimp that are still too young, a group with no adult females, water that keeps changing, too little food, or stress from fish, handling or a new tank. Work through these in turn, and give a stable tank a few weeks before changing anything else.

How long before new shrimp start breeding?

Young shrimp need a few months to mature, and the time varies with temperature, food and line. Adults that are already mature often berry within weeks once they settle, but a recently moved group may pause first.

Do I need a male and a female to breed shrimp?

Yes, but you do not need to pair them. A group of healthy adults in a mixed colony will breed on its own, so start with a decent-sized group rather than counting sexes.

Do shrimp stop breeding if the water is unstable?

They can. Swings in temperature, hardness or other parameters stress shrimp and can delay breeding or make a female drop her eggs. Steady water matters more than chasing a perfect number.

Will Amano shrimp breed in my tank?

Not in a freshwater aquarium. Females may carry eggs, but the larvae need brackish water to develop, so Amano are not bred at home.

My shrimp are berried but no babies appear. Why?

Shrimplets are small and hide at first, so they are easy to miss. If the eggs vanish instead, the female may have dropped them after stress, a molt problem or a sudden change. A steady tank usually lets her berry again.
