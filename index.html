Before you buy

Four things that decide
whether a colony holds.

Hardness, not pH

pH is the reading everyone checks and the one that tells you least. GH and TDS are what a shrimp actually lives in. A TDS meter costs less than five shrimp and saves you more than that in the first month.

Speed of change, not the numbers

Shrimp handle a surprisingly wide range of conditions and almost no movement between them. Drip acclimate over an hour at minimum, and match TDS between your change water and your tank.

Copper, hiding in plain sight

It is in most general fish medications and in some plant fertilizers, and it does not take much. Read the label on anything you dose before it goes near a shrimp tank.

Something is eating the shrimplets

If your colony holds steady but never grows, it is not the water. It is almost always a fish you were told was peaceful. Cover for the babies and a sponge over the filter intake fix most of it.

Before you buy

Which one suits your tank?

The most common expensive mistake in this hobby is buying caridina for a tank that was set up for neocaridina. They are not interchangeable and they cannot share a system.

Most San Diego homes sit in range year round without a heater. Color deepens with a dark substrate, steady feeding and low stress.

Caridina

Intermediate – advanced
pH5.8 – 6.5
GH4 – 6
KH0 – 1
TDS100 – 130
Temperature68 – 74 °F
SubstrateActive / buffering
Water sourceRO + remineralizer

Stability matters more than hitting an exact number. Buffering substrate is not optional — it is what holds the pH down where these shrimp need it.

Do not mix the two

Neocaridina and Caridina need opposite water chemistry. Housing them together means one group is always wrong, and that group will slowly fail to molt. Keep two separate tanks, or pick one.
