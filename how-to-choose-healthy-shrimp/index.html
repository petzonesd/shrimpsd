How to choose healthy shrimp

A few minutes of watching the tank, and a few good questions, help you bring home shrimp that settle in well.

The short answer

In one line

Choose shrimp that are active and grazing, hold steady colour for their type, and come from a tank with clean water and no pile of losses, and make sure your own water suits the type before you take them home.

Shrimp are small and hard to judge at a glance, but a few habits make it much easier. Watch the tank for a minute or two before you choose, look at the group rather than one shrimp, and ask questions. A good seller is glad to answer them.

Signs of a healthy shrimp

Look for shrimp that move around the tank, pick at surfaces with their front legs, and scatter briefly when something startles them. They should cling to plants, moss or decor with ease. The shell should look whole and clean, with colour that is even for the variety. Females carrying eggs under the tail (see breeding and berried shrimp) are usually a good sign that conditions are settled.

Be cautious of shrimp lying on their side, sitting motionless in the open, swimming in odd spirals, or hiding constantly while the rest graze. Shrimp can also look pale for a short time after molting, and a shed shell in the tank is normal, see molting. What matters is the pattern across the group, not one moment.

Look at the tank, not just the shrimp

The tank tells you a lot. Water should look clear and smell clean. Dead shrimp should not be lying around, and several losses in one tank are a reason to wait. Good sellers keep stock in cycled, stable tanks, which is the same approach that works at home, see cycling a shrimp tank.

Pick the right type for your water

Before you choose a colour, choose a type. Neocaridina, such as Cherry shrimp, tolerate dechlorinated tap water and suit most beginners. Caridina, such as Crystal Red shrimp, need soft, acidic water and more care, see Neocaridina vs Caridina and RO water for shrimp. Local tap water matters too, see San Diego tap water. If your tank is not ready, finish the setup first and buy shrimp afterwards.

Questions to ask the seller

Ask which type the shrimp are, what water they have been kept in, how long they have been in the tank, and what they are fed. If you plan to add them to an existing colony, consider a short quarantine. Whatever you choose, acclimate them slowly at home. If you are buying online, our live arrival guarantee page explains how we handle shipping.

Questions we get asked

How can I tell if a shrimp is healthy?

Healthy shrimp move about, graze on surfaces and react when the water is disturbed. Their colour looks steady for the type, their bodies look intact, and they hold on to plants or decor rather than lying on their side. No single sign is proof, so look at the group as a whole.

Is a pale or clear shrimp a problem?

Not always. Some shrimp look paler right after a molt or while they settle in, and some varieties are naturally lighter. Sudden, widespread colour loss together with hiding or not eating is more worrying.

Should I buy shrimp from a tank with dead shrimp in it?

We would avoid it. An occasional loss can happen anywhere, but several dead or struggling shrimp in the same tank are a reason to wait or choose another tank.

Do I need to know what water the shrimp were kept in?

Yes, it helps a lot. Ask what type they are, and what water they have been held in. Neocaridina are comfortable in dechlorinated tap water, while Caridina need soft, acidic water, so match your tank to the shrimp before you buy.

How many shrimp should I start with?

A small group is a better start than a single shrimp, because they are social and easier to read in numbers. See how many shrimp per gallon for stocking, and keep your tank size in mind.
