Shrimp molting explained

What a molt looks like, why you leave the shell, and what a failed molt is telling you.

The short answer

In one line

Molting is how shrimp grow. Leave the shed shell in the tank, keep the water steady, and treat repeated failed molts as a water or mineral problem rather than a disease.

Shrimp wear their skeleton on the outside. To grow they split the old shell, pull free, and harden a new one over the next day or so. It is the most vulnerable moment in a shrimp's life, which is why stable water matters more than perfect numbers.

Step by step

1. Spot the signs

A shrimp about to molt often hides, eats less and may look dull. Do not chase it or net it.

2. Leave the shell alone

After the molt the shrimp is soft and vulnerable for a day or so, and it will often eat the old shell. Keep the shed shell in the tank for a day or two.

3. Keep the water steady

Skip large water changes and any new additives while shrimp are soft. Replace water in small, matched amounts and keep temperature and TDS stable.

4. Check the basics if molts fail

If you see a white ring or repeated failed molts, test GH, KH and TDS against the right range for the species, rule out copper in medications, plant treatments or old fittings, and fix whichever is off slowly.

Normal molt or something wrong?

A normal molt leaves a clean, transparent shell, often split along the back, and a shrimp that is active again within a day. A failed molt shows as a pale ring behind the head, a shrimp that lies on its side, or a shell still attached to the body. Occasional losses happen in any colony; a pattern of them after water changes points to unstable parameters.

Neocaridina in dechlorinated San Diego tap usually have plenty of dissolved minerals. Caridina in RO water need a GH remineralizer, and shrimp in water that is too soft are the ones most likely to struggle. See the water parameter table for the right range per species.

Questions we get asked

Should I remove a shrimp molt from the tank?

No. Shrimp commonly eat their old shell to take back minerals, so leave it for a day or two. Remove it only if it has not been touched after several days and is fouling the water.

How often do shrimp molt?

Young shrimp molt often because they are growing fast. Adults molt less often, roughly every few weeks, and the interval varies with age, temperature and feeding. A shrimp that molts regularly is usually a healthy one.

Why is there a dead-looking shrimp in my tank that is just a shell?

That is almost always a molt. The shed shell, called an exuvia, keeps the shrimp's shape and looks like a pale, transparent copy. A dead shrimp turns opaque, pink or white and does not hold its shape.

What is the white ring of death?

A pale band that appears between the carapace and the abdomen when a shrimp is stuck mid-molt and cannot pull free of the old shell. It is often fatal. It is usually linked to unstable water, a sudden change, low minerals or copper exposure, so look for the cause rather than the shrimp.

Is it bad if several shrimp molt after a water change?

Not by itself. Many keepers see a few shrimp molt after fresh water goes in. It becomes a warning sign when molts are followed by deaths, which points to a swing in parameters. Change water in smaller amounts and match the new water to the tank.
