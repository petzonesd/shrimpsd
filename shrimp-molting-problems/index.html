Shrimp molting problems, and how to fix them

A shrimp grows by climbing out of its old shell and hardening a new one. Most of the time it is invisible and routine. When it goes wrong, it is almost always the water telling you something specific.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

Normal molting first

Healthy shrimp molt every few weeks; young, fast-growing shrimp more often. You will find a perfect, empty, see-through shell lying in the tank and think a shrimp has died. It has not. Leave the shell in — the colony eats it to recover the minerals.

Freshly molted shrimp hide for a day or so while the new shell hardens. Females usually molt just before carrying a new batch of eggs. A burst of molts after a water change is common and normal, as long as they complete.

Signs something is wrong

The white ringA pale band where the head meets the body. The shrimp has started a molt it cannot finish. Usually fatal once visible, but a clear signal to fix the water for the rest of the colony.
Stuck halfwayA shrimp dead partly out of its old shell. Same causes as the white ring.
Soft or deformed shellsToo few minerals to build a proper new shell.
Losses right after water changesThe change was too big or too different from the tank water, triggering molts the shrimp were not ready for.

The causes, and the fixes

1. GH outside the range

GH is the calcium and magnesium in the water — the raw material for a new shell. Too low and shells cannot form; too high and molting becomes labored. Neocaridina want GH 6–8 (tolerating to about 12). Crystal reds and Taiwan bees want GH 4–6; tigers tolerate 4–8. Test it. Full numbers on the parameter reference.

2. Sudden change

A big or mismatched water change shifts GH and TDS overnight, and shrimp mid-cycle molt early or fail. Change 10–20% at a time and match the new water’s TDS and temperature to the tank. The same applies to acclimation: drip slowly, as in how to acclimate shrimp.

3. Infrequent water changes

The opposite problem. If you never change water, minerals get used up and waste builds, then the first big change becomes a shock. Small, regular changes keep conditions steady.

4. Not enough minerals in the diet

Shrimp on RO water get minerals only from what you add back. A remineralizer for the water and a mineral-rich shrimp food or calcium supplement both help. See what do shrimp eat.

5. Toxins

Copper and some medications interfere with molting even at low levels. If failed molts started right after dosing something, that is your answer.

In San Diego

Our tap water is hard — the city’s 2025 report averages roughly GH 13 — which is above the ideal for Neocaridina but usually fine for them once treated for chloramine. If your Neocaridina have molting trouble on straight tap, cutting it roughly half and half with RO often helps. Caridina need RO water remineralized to their range; straight tap will not work. Questions we get asked

What is the white ring of death in shrimp?

A pale band where the head meets the body, showing a shrimp has started a molt it cannot finish. It usually points to GH outside the right range or a sudden change in water chemistry.

Why are my shrimp dying during molting?

Most failed molts come from GH that is too low or too high, or from a sudden change such as a large water change. Test GH, keep changes to 10 to 20 percent, and match the new water to the tank.

Should I remove molted shrimp shells?

No. Leave them in the tank. Shrimp eat the old shell to recover the minerals, and it is usually gone within a day or two.

How often do shrimp molt?

Adult dwarf shrimp molt every few weeks, and young shrimp more often because they are growing quickly.

How do I fix GH for shrimp?

To raise GH, add a GH remineralizer, slowly, over several water changes. To lower it, mix in RO water. Change it gradually; a fast swing causes the molting problems you are trying to fix.
