Temperature guide

Do shrimp need a heater?

Often not, especially here. Dwarf shrimp are not tropical fish, and most San Diego rooms already sit in their range. The bigger risk in this city is heat, not cold.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

The short answer

In one line

Neocaridina are comfortable from 65 to 78°F and Caridina from 68 to 74°F. If your room stays in that range, you do not need a heater. If it drops below on winter nights, a small adjustable heater set around 70–72°F keeps things stable.

Temperature ranges

Neocaridina65–78°F. Cherry, blue dream, bloody mary and every other color morph.
Caridina68–74°F. Crystal red, Taiwan bees and tigers prefer the cooler end and suffer first in heat.
AmanoTolerant across a wide range; comfortable with tropical community temperatures too.

Warmer water speeds up breeding and metabolism but shortens lifespan and holds less oxygen. Cooler water slows everything down. Stability matters more than the exact number: a tank swinging ten degrees between day and night stresses shrimp more than one held a little cool. Full numbers on the parameter reference.

In San Diego

Most homes near the coast sit comfortably in range year round. A heater is worth adding if the tank is in a room that gets cold on winter nights, a garage, or near an exterior door. Keep tanks away from direct sun, air vents and windows, which cause the temperature swings shrimp dislike most.

Inland neighborhoods are the other story. Heat waves can push a tank well past 80°F, where oxygen drops and Caridina start to struggle.

Keeping a shrimp tank cool

  • A fan across the surface. Evaporation cools the water several degrees. Top off with treated water as it evaporates.
  • More surface movement. An air stone or raised sponge filter output adds oxygen when warm water holds less.
  • Lights off or shortened on the hottest days.
  • No ice cubes or big cold water changes. A fast drop is worse than the heat.

If you do use a heater

Pick an adjustable heater with a thermostat sized for the tank, set it around 70–72°F, and check it with a separate thermometer. Shrimp are not harmed by heaters, but shrimplets can hide behind them, so make sure it sits fully submerged where flow passes it.

Questions we get asked

Do cherry shrimp need a heater?

Usually not. Cherry shrimp are comfortable from 65 to 78 degrees Fahrenheit, which most San Diego homes stay within. Add a small heater only if the room drops below that on cold nights.

What temperature do shrimp like?

Neocaridina such as cherry shrimp do well from 65 to 78 degrees Fahrenheit. Caridina such as crystal red shrimp prefer 68 to 74 degrees. Keep it stable rather than chasing an exact number.

Is 80 degrees too hot for shrimp?

It is at the edge for Neocaridina and too warm for Caridina. Warm water holds less oxygen, so add surface movement and a fan across the water during heat waves.

How do I cool down a shrimp tank?

Point a fan across the water surface, increase aeration and shorten the lighting period. Avoid ice or large cold water changes, which cause a sudden drop.
