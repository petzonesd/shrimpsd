Caridina

Tiger shrimp — the easiest Caridina

Striped, characterful and more forgiving than crystal reds or Taiwan bees. Tigers tolerate slightly harder water and a pH nearer neutral, which makes them the sensible first Caridina for anyone stepping across from Neocaridina.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

Which shrimp are “tigers”?

The name covers several different shrimp, and it matters which one you buy:

Super tigerCaridina mariae. Clear body with bold stripes. The most forgiving of the tigers.
Orange eye blue tigerCaridina mariae. Deep blue body, black stripes and orange eyes. More sensitive and pricier; not a first tiger.
Tangerine tigerCaridina serrata. Orange with faint stripes. Hardy and a good starter.
Red and black fancy tigerDespite the name, these are bee-type Caridina cantonensis with a tiger pattern. They need crystal red water, not tiger water.

Water

For true tigers: GH 4–8, KH 0–4, pH 6.5–7.5, TDS 120–180, 68–76°F. That is softer than San Diego tap, which averages roughly GH 13, so start from RO water remineralized to target, or cut tap heavily with RO. Unlike crystal reds, tigers do not strictly need active buffering soil. Full numbers on the parameter reference.

Keep it stable and cool. Tigers are more tolerant than bees, but they are still Caridina: slow changes, careful acclimation, and a fan on hot days. See do shrimp need a heater.

Crossbreeding

Tigers can crossbreed with crystal reds and Taiwan bees. Crystals crossed with tigers produce tibees, and those crossed back produce taitibees. If you want to keep a pure tiger line, keep tigers in their own tank. They will not cross with Neocaridina, but the two need different water, so they should not share a tank anyway. See Neocaridina vs Caridina.

Where tigers fit

Get a Neocaridina colony breeding on its own first. Then a group of super or tangerine tigers is the natural next step before crystal reds and Taiwan bees. More in the Caridina guide.

Questions we get asked

Are tiger shrimp easy to keep?

They are the easiest Caridina, tolerating slightly harder water and a pH nearer neutral than crystal reds. They are still harder than Neocaridina and need soft, stable water.

Can tiger shrimp live in San Diego tap water?

Not straight. San Diego tap averages roughly GH 13, above the tiger range of GH 4 to 8. Use RO water remineralized to target, or tap cut heavily with RO.

Do tiger shrimp breed with cherry shrimp?

No. Tigers are Caridina and cherry shrimp are Neocaridina, so they cannot crossbreed. They do need different water, though, so they should not share a tank.

Do tiger shrimp breed with crystal red shrimp?

Yes. Crystals crossed with tigers produce tibees. Keep tigers separate if you want a pure line.

Are fancy tiger shrimp the same as tiger shrimp?

No. Red and black fancy tigers are bee-type Caridina with a tiger pattern, and they need the same soft, acidic water as crystal reds.
