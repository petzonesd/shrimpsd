Care guide

Baby shrimp care

Shrimplets are tiny, shy and surprisingly tough when the tank is steady. Here is how to help them grow up.

The short answer

In one line

Leave the babies where they hatched, keep the water steady, keep food light and fine, and cover the filter intake. Most shrimplets grow up on their own in a calm, mossy tank.

Neocaridina and Caridina hatch as tiny, fully formed shrimp, with no separate larval stage, so you do not need special equipment to raise them. The job is to give them cover and food they can find, protect them from the filter, and avoid sudden changes. For the stage before this, see berried shrimp and breeding.

What helps shrimplets most

1. Cover. Java moss and other fine plants give babies somewhere to hide and graze. Leaf litter and botanicals add more surface for biofilm. See live plants for shrimp and catappa leaves and botanicals.

2. A covered filter intake. Use a sponge filter or fit a sponge pre-filter so babies are not pulled in. See filters for shrimp tanks.

3. Light feeding. A mature tank grows biofilm for them. Offer only very fine food in small amounts, since leftovers rot and foul the water. See feeding shrimp.

4. Steady water. Keep temperature and chemistry stable and make water changes small and slow, with chloramine treated every time. See water changes and tank temperature.

5. No fish and no copper. Most fish will eat shrimplets, and copper harms young shrimp. See tank mates and medications and copper.

Species notes

Neocaridina are the forgiving choice and their babies do well in dechlorinated San Diego tap that is kept steady. Caridina babies need the same soft, acidic, low-mineral water as their parents, made with RO and a remineralizer, and they tolerate swings less well. See Neocaridina vs Caridina and RO water for Caridina.

Questions we get asked

Do I need to separate baby shrimp from the adults?

Usually not. Adult Neocaridina and Caridina generally leave their young alone, so the babies can stay in the main tank. Moss, leaf litter and other cover help them hide and graze, and moving them stresses them more than it helps.

What do baby shrimp eat?

Mostly biofilm and tiny bits that settle on moss, leaves and surfaces. A mature, planted tank often feeds them without help. If you add food, use very fine powdered shrimp food in small amounts and skip it if you see leftovers, since rotting food fouls the water.

Why can't I see any baby shrimp?

They are very small and stay hidden at first, usually in moss, plants and the gaps between decor. Look closely around moss with the lights low and you will often spot them grazing.

Can baby shrimp get sucked into the filter?

Yes. Shrimplets are small enough to be pulled into an uncovered intake. A sponge filter, or a sponge pre-filter over the intake of another filter, keeps them safe. See our filter guide for shrimp-safe setups.

Do baby shrimp need different water from adults?

No, they need the same species-appropriate water, kept steady. Large or fast changes in temperature or water chemistry hit small shrimp hardest, so make water changes small and slow, and always treat tap water for chloramine.

How long until baby shrimp are adults?

It takes a few months for them to mature, and the time varies with temperature, food and the line. Young shrimp can often be sexed only once they get a little older; see our male vs female guide.

Are baby shrimp sensitive to copper and medications?

Yes, young shrimp are at least as sensitive as adults. Avoid copper and fish medications in a tank with shrimp or shrimplets, and check plants and products for copper or pesticides before adding them.
