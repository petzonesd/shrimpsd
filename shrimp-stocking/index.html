How many shrimp per gallon

How many to start with, and how much a tank can hold.

The short answer

In one line

Start with a small group of about 6 to 10, keep the tank stable, and let the colony grow itself instead of stocking to the limit on day one.

Shrimp are tiny and make very little waste individually, so a small tank can hold a surprising number. The catch is that Neocaridina often breed readily, so the count rises on its own. Stocking lightly at the start leaves room for that.

How many to start with

Shrimp kept alone or in pairs tend to hide. A group of about 6 to 10 feels safer to them, shows color better and gives you a mix for breeding. Make sure the tank is cycled first; a new tank with no established filter bacteria cannot handle even a modest group.

How many a tank can hold

There is no single correct number per gallon. As a rough guide, many keepers stay at or below about 5 Neocaridina per gallon in a mature, well-planted tank, and keep Caridina well under that because they are less tolerant of poor water. Treat this as a ceiling to work toward slowly, not a target. The tank’s real limit depends on the filter, plants and moss, feeding and how often you do water changes.

Signs you have too many

Watch for shrimp lingering at the surface, ammonia or nitrite on a test kit, rising nitrate between water changes, uneaten food, or unexplained deaths. If you see these, test the water, do a gentle water change and stop adding shrimp until things settle. See why shrimp die for a fuller checklist.

Mixing species

Do not mix Neocaridina with Caridina in one tank because their water needs differ; see Neocaridina vs Caridina. Different Neocaridina colors will interbreed, and the young usually turn out in plain, wild-type colors. Fish count toward the load too; see tank mates.

Questions we get asked

How many shrimp should I start with?

A group of about 6 to 10 is a good start for a small tank. Shrimp are social and tend to hide when kept in ones and twos, and a starter group lets the colony breed on its own.

How many shrimp per gallon is too many?

There is no exact number. As a rough guide, many keepers stay at or below about 5 Neocaridina per gallon in a mature, well-planted tank, and keep Caridina well under that. Watch water tests and shrimp behavior, not just the count.

Will my shrimp colony grow by itself?

Neocaridina often breed readily once settled, so the colony can grow faster than expected. Start modestly and add more only if the tank has been stable for a while.

Can I add all my shrimp at once?

Adding a small group at once is fine for a cycled tank, and acclimating them slowly matters more than spacing out arrivals. Do not add many shrimp at once to a new or just-cycled tank, because the filter has to catch up with their waste.

What happens if a shrimp tank is overstocked?

Waste and ammonia or nitrite rise, oxygen falls, and shrimp become stressed, which can show as hiding, swimming at the surface or sudden deaths. Test the water, do a gentle water change and stop adding shrimp.
