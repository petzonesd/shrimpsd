Neocaridina davidi

Bloody mary shrimp

The deepest, most glassy red in Neocaridina. Same easy care as a cherry, with color that holds up better under stress — which is why it is the red most people trade up to.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

What makes a bloody mary different

Bloody mary is a separate line, not a higher grade of cherry. In cherry, sakura and fire red shrimp, the red comes from pigment that varies in coverage across the body. In bloody mary, the red sits in the flesh beneath a clear shell, giving a deep, almost translucent ruby color that stays strong even when the shrimp is stressed or freshly molted.

Bloody maryDeep, glassy red throughout the body, including under the shell. Color holds well through stress.
Painted fire redSolid opaque red on the shell, including the legs. Can pale when stressed.
Cherry / sakuraPatchier, more translucent red. The hardiest and cheapest.

For grading across the cherry line, see red cherry shrimp.

Water and setup

The same as any Neocaridina: GH 6–8 (tolerating to about 12), KH 2–8, pH 6.5–8.0, TDS 150–250, 65–78°F. In San Diego that means treated tap water works; it runs harder than this range, and cutting it roughly half and half with RO can deepen the color. The water is chloraminated, so treat every drop. Full details on San Diego tap water.

Five gallons will hold a colony and ten is easier. Sponge filter, moss for the babies, dark substrate to show off the red.

Keeping the line true

  • One color per tank. Bloody mary will crossbreed with any other Neocaridina, and within a few generations the offspring drift toward brown wild-type.
  • Cull the pale ones if color matters to you. Move them to another tank rather than letting them breed back in.
  • Dark substrate and plants. Every red shrimp looks better over black soil and against green moss.

Questions we get asked

What is the difference between bloody mary and cherry shrimp?

They are the same species, but bloody mary is a separate line. Its red sits in the flesh beneath a clear shell, giving a deeper, glassier color that holds up better under stress than cherry, sakura or fire red grades.

Are bloody mary shrimp hard to keep?

No. Care is identical to cherry shrimp. In San Diego they can live on treated tap water, and they breed readily in a stable, planted tank.

Can bloody mary shrimp live with cherry shrimp?

They can share a tank safely, but they will interbreed. Over a few generations the colors blend and drift toward brown wild-type, so keep one color per tank if you want to keep the line.

Do bloody mary shrimp breed true?

Mostly. A good line produces mostly deep red offspring with some paler ones. Removing the pale shrimp keeps the line strong.
