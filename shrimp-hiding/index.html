Care guide

Why are my shrimp hiding?

Hiding is often normal shrimp behavior. What matters is whether they come out to graze again, and whether anything else looks wrong.

The short answer

In one line

Shrimp hide when they are new, molting or lit too brightly. If they come out to graze within a day or two and look healthy, it is usually fine; if hiding comes with other problems, check the water.

Shrimp are small and prey for almost everything, so staying out of sight is a built-in habit. A colony in a mature, planted tank tends to spread out and graze in the open. A new colony in a bare tank often does not.

Common normal reasons

A new home. Moving shrimp to a new tank is stressful even when the water is right. After acclimating them slowly, expect them to hide for a day or more and then explore. Avoid chasing or lifting them to check.

Molting. A shrimp with a soft new shell hides until it hardens. A shed shell nearby is the clue. See shrimp molting; leave the shell in the tank, as the shrimp will often eat it.

Bright, bare surroundings. Shrimp feel exposed in a bright tank with little cover. Live plants, moss, driftwood and leaves give them places to retreat to, see plants for shrimp and botanicals. A moderate light on a timer helps.

Fish. Even peaceful fish can unsettle shrimp, and some will hunt them. If the hiding began when a fish was added, read tank mates.

When hiding is a warning

Pay attention when hiding comes with several shrimp that are not eating, lie on their sides, swim in frantic circles or turn up dead. These point to water quality or exposure problems rather than shyness. Check the filter is running, and look back over what changed: a large water change, untreated tap water, a new product or any medication, especially one that might contain copper, see medications and copper. Compare your numbers with water parameters and temperature, then follow why shrimp die.

How to help shy shrimp

Keep things steady rather than adding fixes. Provide cover, keep the light moderate and regular, feed lightly as described in feeding shrimp, and let the tank mature. Small, calm routines do more than frequent tinkering. If you are adding new shrimp to an existing colony, a short quarantine keeps both groups safer.

Questions we get asked

Is it normal for shrimp to hide?

Yes, especially after a move, before or after a molt, and in a bright, open tank. Healthy shrimp that hide for a day or two and then graze again are usually fine. Hiding together with not eating, lying on their side or dying shrimp points to a water or health problem.

Why are my new shrimp hiding?

A new tank, new water and handling are stressful. Many new shrimp stay out of sight for a day or more, then come out once they settle. Dim the light a little, keep the tank quiet and give them cover such as plants and moss.

Why do shrimp hide when they molt?

A freshly molted shrimp has a soft shell and is vulnerable, so it tucks away until the new shell hardens. Seeing a shrimp hide briefly and a shed shell nearby is normal.

Can bright light make shrimp hide?

Yes. Shrimp in a bare, brightly lit tank often stay hidden. Plants, moss, driftwood and a lighting timer with a moderate intensity give them places to feel secure and encourage grazing.

When is hiding a warning sign?

When it comes with several shrimp not eating, lying on their sides, swimming frantically or dying. Test the water, check for chloramine or copper exposure and look at recent changes. Our guide on why shrimp die covers the next steps.
