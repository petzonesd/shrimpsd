Feeding guide

What do shrimp eat?

Mostly, something you cannot see. Dwarf shrimp spend their day grazing biofilm, the thin film of algae, bacteria and microorganisms that coats every surface in a mature tank. Prepared food is a supplement to that, not a replacement for it.

By the shrimp desk at Pet Zone Convoy, San Diego · Updated October 8, 2026

The short answer

In one line

Let biofilm do most of the work, feed a small amount of shrimp food every day or two, offer leaf litter and the occasional blanched vegetable, and remove anything the colony has not finished in two to three hours.

What shrimp eat, in order of importance

BiofilmThe main diet. It grows on glass, hardscape, plants and leaves, and it is why a tank running six months supports far more shrimp than the same tank at six weeks. Shrimplets live on it almost entirely.
Leaf litter and botanicalsCatappa (Indian almond) leaves, alder cones and cholla wood break down slowly and grow biofilm as they go. Leave leaves in until they are skeletons.
Prepared shrimp foodA staple pellet or stick made for shrimp, with the minerals they need for molting. This fills the gaps biofilm leaves.
Blanched vegetablesZucchini, spinach, kale and cucumber, briefly boiled to soften and sink. Pesticide-free or well washed, and out of the tank within a few hours.
Protein, occasionallyShrimp scavenge in the wild and will eat a bit of fish food or a protein-based shrimp food. Once a week is plenty; more fouls the water.

How much and how often

Feed an amount the colony clears in about two to three hours, every day or every other day. For ten to fifteen shrimp that is often a single small pellet. Skip a day or two a week; a healthy colony will not notice, and the tank will be cleaner for it.

A good sign you have it right: shrimp swarm the food within minutes, then go back to grazing. If food sits untouched, you are feeding too much or the tank already has plenty of biofilm. Pull leftovers out with a pipette or turkey baster rather than letting them rot.

Going on vacation? An established shrimp tank can go a week or more without being fed. It is far safer than an automatic feeder or a neighbor with a heavy hand.

Feeding shrimplets

Babies cannot push through adults for a pellet, so they rely on what is spread across the tank. A powdered biofilm supplement dusted over moss and leaves, plus plenty of leaf litter, makes a real difference to how many survive their first month.

Minerals: the part people miss

Shrimp build a new shell every few weeks, and that takes calcium and other minerals from the water and the food. In San Diego, tap water is already hard, so Neocaridina on treated tap rarely need extra. Shrimp on RO water need it added back with a remineralizer, and a calcium supplement or mineral-rich food helps when molts look difficult. See water parameters for the GH targets.

Feeding mistakes that crash tanks

  • Overfeeding. The most common one. Uneaten food rots into ammonia, and small tanks have no margin for it.
  • Medicated fish foods. Some contain treatments that are unsafe for invertebrates. Use foods made for shrimp or labeled invertebrate safe.
  • Unwashed vegetables. Pesticide residue on produce can kill a colony. Wash well or buy organic.
  • Leaving vegetables in overnight. They break down fast and cloud the water.

Questions we get asked

What do cherry shrimp eat?

Mostly biofilm, the film of algae and microorganisms that grows on every surface in a mature tank. Add a small amount of shrimp food every day or two, leaf litter such as catappa leaves, and the occasional blanched vegetable.

How often should I feed shrimp?

Every day or every other day, in an amount the colony finishes in two to three hours. Skipping a day or two a week is fine and keeps the water cleaner.

Can shrimp eat fish food?

Yes, they will scavenge fish food, and a little is fine. Avoid medicated fish foods, and do not rely on fish food alone, since shrimp foods carry the minerals they need to molt.

How long can shrimp go without food?

An established tank with plenty of biofilm can go a week or more without feeding. That is safer than overfeeding while you are away.

What vegetables can shrimp eat?

Blanched zucchini, spinach, kale and cucumber are all good. Wash them well to remove pesticides and take them out within a few hours.
