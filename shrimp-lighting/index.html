Lighting a shrimp tank

Shrimp do not need strong light. A steady schedule, moderate brightness and plenty of cover matter more than any particular lamp.

The short answer

In one line

Run a moderate light on a timer for roughly six to eight hours a day, give the tank a dark night, and add plants and cover so shrimp feel safe enough to graze in the open.

The shrimp are not the main reason to light the tank. Light feeds live plants and the biofilm and soft algae that shrimp graze on, and it lets you enjoy the colony. Too much of it mostly produces algae and shy shrimp.

How long and how bright

Use a timer. A plug-in timer keeps the day length steady and removes the temptation to leave the light on. Many keepers settle on about six to eight hours a day; start at the low end and extend only if plants need it.

Keep it moderate. There is no single brightness number that suits every tank, because it depends on the lamp, the depth and the plants. If the shrimp spend the day hidden in a bare tank, the light is probably too strong or there is too little cover. Floating plants, driftwood and moss soften the light and give shrimp somewhere to retreat, see plants for shrimp.

Give them night. A regular dark period is normal for shrimp. Avoid leaving the light on around the clock.

Light and algae

Excess light is a common driver of algae, especially in a young tank where plants are still small. If algae is spreading, shorten the light period slightly, move the light higher or add floating plants before reaching for any treatment, and check the food you add. Many algae products can harm shrimp, see medications and copper and algae in a shrimp tank.

Natural light and room light

A tank near a window may get enough daylight for some hardy plants, but direct sun tends to cause algae and can warm the water. Watch the thermometer, see shrimp tank temperature. If shrimp are hiding more than you expect, read why shrimp hide.

Questions we get asked

Do shrimp need a light?

Shrimp themselves do not need bright light, but a regular day and night rhythm is good for them, and live plants and the algae and biofilm shrimp graze on need light to grow. A tank in a room with some natural daylight may need little or no extra light for shrimp alone.

How many hours a day should I run the light?

Many keepers run a shrimp tank light for roughly six to eight hours a day on a timer. More light mainly feeds algae. Consistency matters more than the exact number, so pick a schedule and keep it steady.

Is bright light bad for shrimp?

Strong light is not toxic, but in a bare tank it makes shrimp feel exposed and they tend to hide. It also encourages algae. Moderate light with plants, moss and driftwood for cover works better for most colonies.

Do shrimp need darkness at night?

Yes, a dark period each day suits them and is easy to provide with a timer. Avoid leaving a tank light on around the clock.

Do shrimp need a UV or special aquarium light?

No. Any ordinary aquarium light that suits your plants is fine for shrimp. Choose by what your plants need, and turn the intensity down or float plants on the surface if the tank looks too bright.
