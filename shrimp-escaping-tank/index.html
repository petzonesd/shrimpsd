Shrimp jumping out of the tank

Shrimp climb and jump more than most people expect. Here is why it happens, what it can mean for your water, and how to keep them in.

The short answer

In one line

A snug lid with the gaps around cords and tubing closed is the simplest prevention, and a shrimp that keeps trying to leave is often telling you something is off in the water, so test it as well as covering the tank.

Shrimp are good climbers, and a startled shrimp can flick its tail and launch itself over the rim. Most escapes happen in the first nights after new shrimp go in, after a water change, or when something in the water is wrong.

Why shrimp leave the water

There are a few common reasons, and they are worth sorting out because they call for different fixes.

  • Exploring. Newly added shrimp roam a lot while they learn the tank, and some will follow plants, wood, or a filter pipe straight up and out.
  • Poor water quality. Ammonia, nitrite, low oxygen, chloramine left in the water, or copper can all make shrimp head for the surface and the glass above the waterline. If many shrimp are doing this at once, treat it as a warning. See why shrimp die and chloramine and shrimp.
  • Low oxygen. Warm water holds less oxygen, and a still surface does not help. Our guide to air stones and oxygen covers the signs.
  • A sudden change. A water change that shifts temperature or TDS quickly, or an acclimation that was rushed, can spook shrimp. The drip method and gentler water changes help.
  • Molting and mating activity. When a female molts, males often swim around the tank searching for her, and an excited group can wander toward the surface. See molting and breeding.

Where they get out

Check the places a shrimp can squeeze or climb through: the cut-out for the filter, gaps around the heater cord or airline tubing, the space between a lid and the back of the tank, and any plant, moss or hardscape that touches the rim. A filter outflow or intake pipe that rises above the water line works as a ladder. In open-top or rimless tanks the water level itself matters, since a tank filled to the brim is easy to leave.

How to escape-proof the tank

  1. Add a lid. A glass lid or a fitted cover with small gaps is the best single step. Make sure it still allows some air exchange.
  2. Close the gaps. Fill cut-outs around cords, pipes and tubing with aquarium-safe foam or fine mesh, and keep any intake covered with a sponge pre-filter. See filters for shrimp tanks.
  3. Lower the water slightly. Keep the waterline a little below the rim so a shrimp has to climb, not just hop.
  4. Trim the bridges. Cut back plants, moss and wood that reach the rim or hang over it.
  5. Watch the first nights. Check the tank, and the floor and cabinet around it, in the first few days after adding shrimp or doing a big maintenance job.

If a shrimp is found out of the tank

Pick it up gently, ideally with a wet hand or a small cup, and put it back into the tank water right away. A shrimp that was out only briefly and is still moist can recover, though a shrimp that has dried out usually cannot be saved. Do not skip the next step: test the water and look at the rest of the colony, because a single escape is sometimes the first sign of a water problem. If others are climbing, gasping or lethargic, work through our troubleshooting guide.

What not to do

Do not seal the tank airtight, since shrimp and the filter bacteria both need oxygen. Do not use foam, mesh or sealants that are not aquarium-safe, and do not treat the water with medications or additives without checking they are safe for invertebrates; see medications and copper. Do not rely on a lid alone if shrimp keep trying to leave, because the cause may be in the water.

Questions we get asked

Can shrimp jump out of a tank?

Yes. Shrimp can climb pipes, plants and equipment, and a startled shrimp can flick itself over the rim. A lid with the gaps around cords and tubing closed prevents most escapes.

Why are my shrimp climbing to the top of the tank?

A few climbing now and then is usually exploring. Many shrimp gathering at the surface or on the glass above the waterline can point to ammonia, nitrite, low oxygen, chloramine or copper, so test the water.

Do shrimp escape more after a water change?

They can. A change in temperature, TDS or pH can unsettle shrimp, and new shrimp are especially restless. Match the new water closely and add it slowly.

Does a lid hurt shrimp tanks?

Not if it leaves some air exchange. A lid with small gaps keeps shrimp in while still letting oxygen and gases move. Do not seal the tank airtight.

Can a shrimp that jumped out survive?

Sometimes. If it was out only briefly and is still moist, put it back in the tank water right away and it may recover. A shrimp that has dried out usually cannot be saved.
